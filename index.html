Yinjie Yu

Ph.D. Candidate in Economics — Finance and Macroeconomics
Princeton University

Welcome! I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Economics at Princeton University, where I study financial economics and macroeconomics. I expect to complete my Ph.D. in June 2027.

My research studies how central bank balance sheet policy shapes the pricing and sustainability of government debt. I ask when quantitative easing transmits to inflation and to sovereign credit spreads, how the maturity structure of public debt alters that transmission, and how purchase and exit policies should be designed once banks operate under post-crisis liquidity regulation.

My approach is structural. I build general equilibrium models in which no-arbitrage bond pricing sits alongside financial intermediaries facing regulatory constraints, and I characterize the policy problem the central bank solves as a Ramsey problem — which makes it possible to say not only what balance sheet policy does, but what it should do.

Before Princeton I received a B.A. in Economics, with a minor in Mathematics and Applied Mathematics, from Peking University.

Feel free to reach out if you are interested in my work or would like to discuss a project.
