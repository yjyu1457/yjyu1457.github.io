Yinjie Yu

Ph.D. Candidate in Economics — Finance and Macroeconomics
Princeton University

Welcome! I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Economics at Princeton University, where I study financial economics and macroeconomics. I expect to complete my Ph.D. in February 2027.

My research asks how central bank balance sheet policy interacts with the pricing of government debt. A recurring theme is equilibrium multiplicity: in both of my main projects, identical fundamentals and policy rules support more than one rational-expectations equilibrium, so what quantitative easing actually does to inflation and to sovereign credit spreads depends on which equilibrium investors have coordinated on. I work on the theory of bond pricing under the fiscal theory of the price level, the seniority of reserves in the government's consolidated balance sheet, and liquidity regulation as a constraint on monetary transmission.

Since 2021 I have been a research assistant to Moritz Lenel at the Bendheim Center for Finance, working on segmented-markets term structure models and heterogeneous-agent asset pricing. I have also spent time in policy and in industry — at the International Monetary Fund's European Department and as a quantitative researcher at IMC Trading and Scientech Research.

Before Princeton I received a B.A. in Economics, with a minor in Mathematics and Applied Mathematics, from Peking University.

Feel free to reach out if you are interested in my work or would like to discuss a project.
