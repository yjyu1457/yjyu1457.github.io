  • Self-Fulfilling Inflation Risk Premia under the Fiscal Theory: Equilibrium Multiplicity and Government Debt Maturity
    Yinjie Yu
    Working paper, 2026
    I study self-fulfilling inflation risk premia within the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level. In a New Keynesian model with an affine pricing kernel, the no-arbitrage bond-pricing equation admits two rational-expectations equilibria under the same structural parameters, Taylor rule, and fiscal rule: a Fundamental Equilibrium in which fiscal uncertainty is unpriced, and a Fiscal Sunspot Equilibrium in which investors who coordinate on pricing fiscal uncertainty bid up the real value of government debt. In the Fiscal Sunspot Equilibrium nominal bonds are a fiscal safe haven: they carry a negative fiscal risk premium and a negative inflation risk premium, and a shock to fiscal uncertainty produces an impact deflation followed by a hump-shaped inflation response. Government debt maturity acts asymmetrically on the two channels — longer duration dampens the passthrough of fundamental real-rate shocks to inflation while amplifying the self-fulfilling fiscal channel. The asymmetry has a direct policy consequence: a maturity shortening of the kind induced by quantitative easing is substantially less disinflationary when fiscal uncertainty is priced than when it is not — roughly half as much at the baseline calibration — and reverses sign outright once the fiscal loading is large enough. The effectiveness of debt maturity management is therefore not a structural constant but depends on which equilibrium investors have coordinated on, with identical fundamentals, policy rules, and states.

  • Reserves Expansion and Self-Fulfilling Debt Crisis
    Yinjie Yu
    Working paper · JEL: E58, G12, H63
    I study how central bank balance sheet policies influence the credit spread of sovereign debt. In my model, multiple debt price equilibria arise due to investors' self-fulfilling beliefs about the future government debt path. Quantitative easing (QE), by increasing the supply of reserves — which are more senior liabilities — in exchange for long-term debt, dilutes existing bondholders and can trigger a shift to the default equilibrium if implemented as a one-time unexpected policy. In contrast, a credible commitment to expand the balance sheet in response to rising debt can anchor expectations and eliminate the default equilibrium by moderating the perceived future debt trajectory.

  • A Model of Optimal QE and QT with Segmented Bond Market and Liquidity Regulations
    Yinjie Yu
    Working paper · Princeton third-year paper
    This paper analyzes optimal asset-purchase and exit policies in a macroeconomic model with banks facing precautionary liquidity regulations (Liquidity Stress Testing and Leverage Coverage Ratio) and balance sheet costs. QE accelerates recoveries after productivity, liquidity, and bank net worth shocks by inflating bond prices and restoring bank net worth. It becomes more powerful as it provides reserves and boosts intermediation when the LCR constraint binds. The optimal exit strategy is gradual whenever the LCR constraint binds. However, a fast QT is costless and expansionary when liquidity is abundant as it drives the banks to substitute bonds for working capital.
